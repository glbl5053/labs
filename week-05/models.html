Week 05 · Model overview

A map of the model list.

On October 5, 2026, OpenRouter listed 465 models from 57 organizations. You do not need to know them. You need to be able to read one entry, and to know roughly where it sits among the rest.

This page will go out of date. Every price and model name below was read from OpenRouter's public catalog on October 5, 2026. Names change monthly. The way to read them changes slowly, and that is what this page is for. The Week 5 notebook reads live prices each time it runs.

First, what the list is

OpenRouter is a broker. It makes none of these models.

Anthropic, Google, OpenAI, Alibaba, Mistral and the rest each run their own models behind their own APIs, with their own accounts and billing. OpenRouter sits in front of them: one key, one request format, one bill. You name a model, and it forwards your request to a company that hosts it.

That is why one course key can reach all of them, and why the list is so long. It is close to a census of what is commercially available, which makes it a useful thing to learn to read even if you never use OpenRouter again.

Reading one entry

A model ID is an address: who made it, then which one.

Take anthropic/claude-sonnet-5.5. Before the slash is the organization. After it is the product family (Claude), the size class within that family (Sonnet), and a version number, where higher means newer.

  • Size words. Each lab has its own, but they read the same way. In the catalog today, Anthropic prices Fable above Opus above Sonnet above Haiku. OpenAI's GPT-6 line runs Astra, Sol, Luna. Google's runs Pro, Flash, Flash-Lite. Elsewhere, max, pro and large sit above flash, mini, lite and nano.
  • A number followed by b is the model's size in billions of parameters: gemma-4-31b-it has about 31 billion, llama-3.2-1b-instruct about one billion. Labs that keep their models private usually do not publish this number.
  • Suffixes after a colon. :free costs nothing but is rate-limited. :batch is the same model at about half price for jobs that can wait. Neither is needed for this course.
  • A leading tilde, as in ~openai/gpt-luna-latest, is an alias that follows the newest version in a family.

What to check

Five facts tell you most of what you need.

  1. What goes in and what comes out. Most entries take text and return text. Many also accept images or files; some accept audio or video. A few return images or audio. This decides whether a model can do your task at all.
  2. Price, twice. Dollars per million tokens of input, and a higher figure per million tokens of output. A token is about three-quarters of a word. Output prices in the catalog run from $0.03 to $600 per million.
  3. Context length. How many tokens the model can take in at once, counting your documents and the whole conversation. Around a million is now common at the top; small models range from 60,000 to about 260,000.
  4. Whether it reasons first. Many current models write out private working before they answer. It often improves hard answers, it takes longer, and those tokens are billed as output. For 116 of the 465 entries it cannot be switched off.
  5. Release date. A model from 2024 at the same price as one from 2026 is rarely the better buy. Age also bounds what the model can know about the world without being given documents.

Reading the map

Three things worth noticing before you run anything.

The range is enormous. From the top row to the bottom, the price of output falls by a factor of about 250, and the model shrinks from a size its maker does not disclose to one billion parameters. For scale, the GPT built in this week's video has about 10 million, and the smallest GPT-2 has 124 million.

Price and the benchmark do not line up neatly. The two most expensive models in the table do not have the highest index; two cheaper Anthropic models score above both. And a $0.50 model scores close to a $3.75 one. A single number compresses many tests into one, sellers set prices for their own reasons, and neither tells you how a model handles your task. That is the reason to compare them yourself.

The drop is not smooth. On this index the first twelve rows fall between 38 and 58; the last five are all below 15. Whether that cliff shows up in your own prompts, and where, is what the notebook's experiments are for.

Who is on the list

A handful of labs account for most entries.

Counting every listed variant, OpenAI has 102 entries, Alibaba's Qwen 54, Google 41, Anthropic 29, Mistral 25, Z.ai 18, and DeepSeek 15. Many of these are older versions kept available.

  • United States: OpenAI, Google, Anthropic, xAI (the Grok models, listed as SpaceXAI), Meta, NVIDIA, Amazon, Microsoft, IBM.
  • China: Alibaba (Qwen), Z.ai (GLM), DeepSeek, Moonshot (Kimi), MiniMax, Tencent, ByteDance, Xiaomi, Baidu.
  • Elsewhere: Mistral in France, Cohere in Canada, Upstage in South Korea, Sakana in Japan.

The notebook's EXTRAS list includes models from several of these so you can put them side by side on the same prompt.

A distinction that matters for policy

Closed models and open-weight models.

A closed model stays on its maker's servers; you can only send it requests. Claude Fable 5.1 is reachable through four endpoints: Anthropic itself and three cloud partners.

An open-weight model's parameters are published, so anyone with enough hardware can download and run it, modify it, or host it for others. About 180 of the 465 entries link to published weights. DeepSeek V4.1 Flash is offered through 30 endpoints and GLM 5.3 Flash through 33, most of them independent hosting companies.

The tiny models at the bottom of the table are all open-weight, and small enough to run on a laptop or phone with no API at all. When you read a proposal to control access to AI models, it is worth asking which of these two kinds it could actually reach.

Beyond chat

Not every model returns words.

Embedding models turn a text into a list of numbers so that texts can be compared and searched by meaning. OpenRouter lists 33 of them separately; openai/text-embedding-3-small is the one in the notebook's optional section.

Image and audio models return pictures or sound, for example google/gemini-3.1-flash-lite-image and openai/gpt-audio-mini. They use the same request pattern with different content in the messages.

Routers such as openrouter/auto choose a model for you. Convenient in a product; unhelpful when the point is to know which model said what.

Choosing

Three questions, in this order.

  1. What goes in and what must come out? This removes most of the list.
  2. How many times will this run? Once, by hand: price barely matters, so use a strong model. Ten thousand times: price is the decision, so find the cheapest model that passes your own test.
  3. What happens if it is wrong? If a mistake is cheap to catch, start small and move up only when you see failures. If it is costly, start at the top and check the output anyway.
